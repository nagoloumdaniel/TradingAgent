research: the sweep did cross 1,0 once, and splitting the window is what killed it

My previous message said nothing crosses 1,0 on the full dataset. That was wrong, and the
correction matters more than the original claim.

stop 1,5 ATR with targets at 3,0 and 6,0 and the partial exit does cross: 694 trades, 26,8 % of
wins, +39,85 EUR, profit factor 1,0073. It falls twice.

By the window: 0,9190 on the first 30 000 bars against 1,1016 on the second. The 1,0073 is the
average of one losing period and one winning one, which is not an edge but a calendar. The
control configuration, split the same way, gives 0,9326 and 0,9333 -- stable.

By the cost: under the observed spread of 18.424 USD it drops to 0,9446 and a net of -310,49 EUR.
Under that spread not one of the seven measured configurations stays above 1,0.

So the sweep was right to answer no, and it answered no for a reason worth keeping: with sixteen
configurations measured on one window, the best is biased upward by construction, and that bias
was enough to manufacture a threshold crossing. Evaluating each candidate on both halves is what
turned 1,0073 into a no. That method is the deliverable as much as the table is.

The premise I gave this axis is refuted, and in the opposite direction. Moving the targets
further away raises the factor monotonically -- 0,8362 to 0,9314 to 0,9506 to 1,0073 -- while
tightening the stop makes it collapse, 0,7422 at 0,5 ATR with a net of -2876,58 EUR, the worst
row in the sweep. The best stop is 1,2 ATR with a plateau from 1,2 to 2,0. The useful gradient
runs through distant targets, not through the stop, and I had argued the opposite from a median
MAE that could not support it.

The structural trailing stop, built earlier today and never tested on this rule, helps the
operator's geometry at strength 2, 0,8553 against 0,8362 and +144,43 EUR, and hurts at strength
3, 0,8247. One geometry and two points are a lead, not a result, and the report says so.

Two measurement practices earned their place. Carrying the harness fingerprint in every row:
the harness changed twice during the session, once mid-sweep, and without that field the sixteen
rows could not be shown to be mutually consistent. And splitting the window: it is the only
reason this report says no instead of publishing 1,0073.

Reported without dressing: six configurations of the crossing were not measured on the full
dataset, and one confounder stays unneutralised -- max_concurrent_positions is 1, so a tighter
stop frees the slot sooner and the number of executed trades varies from 883 to 1436 for a
strictly constant signal set. Two rows therefore do not compare the same trades, and the report
says which.
